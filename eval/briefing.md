# Kestrel Valley Link Road: project briefing for query writers

Read this instead of the documents. Then write search queries the way a project participant
would: from memory, in your own words, without looking at filenames, document codes or folders.
Everything here is fictional.

## The project

A 4.2 km single-carriageway bypass for the market town of Kestrel Valley, built from spring 2025.
It includes a three-span bridge over the River Kest, a box culvert under the road at Mill Lane,
two attenuation ponds (Pond 1 West and Pond 2 East), a four-arm roundabout at the eastern end,
and a tie-in to the existing Kestrel Road.

## What happened

**Design, 2024.** Stratum's ground investigation for the bridge came out in October 2024. One
late borehole at the east abutment found a lens of soft clay, but that result sat in an
appendix. A drainage strategy for both ponds was issued in summer 2024 and updated that winter;
the council accepted it in January 2025. Drawings for the bridge, culvert, ponds, drainage
layout and roundabout were issued for construction around March 2025, together with a tender
bill of quantities for the pavements and the contractor's baseline programme.

**East abutment piles.** Piling at the east abutment began in June 2025 and hit very soft clay
well below where the piles were meant to stop. It came up at a fortnightly progress meeting in
mid-June, and the contractor raised a formal question to the designer. The designer replied by
letter at the end of June: the piles were lengthened from 18 m to 24 m. Revised drawings and a
new pile schedule followed in July, the independent checker reissued its certificate, and the
contractor submitted a piling method statement, which was approved with comments in August.
The contractor also asked a follow-up question about the pile cut-off level on the revised
layout. Much later, in October, the designer sent round another version of the abutment
drawing just for comment, proposing a drainage layer; the July drawing remained the one to
build from.

**Mill Lane culvert and the water main.** Trial holes in June found the existing water main
lower than the utility records showed, too close to the culvert roof. The council wrote to
Northvale Water with the results, and the contractor raised a formal question about the clash.
Northvale Water replied in July with its clearance rules. The designer answered by lowering the
culvert and adding a concrete protection slab over the main. Revised drawings came out in
August after an independent check, and the contractor priced the extra work as a variation of
roughly fifty thousand pounds, which the council accepted in September. Northvale Water
formally accepted the slab in early September.

**Pond 2 East.** In May 2025 the council told the designer that an updated flood model meant
Pond 2 could only discharge 5 litres per second instead of 8. The designer produced a new report
in July that replaced the old drainage strategy, enlarging the pond. The contractor submitted a
flow control device, approved with comments in September. In October they tried to swap in a
cheaper device; it was rejected because it let too much water through, so the original device
stands. The enlarged pond drawings came out in late October. There's also a planting plan for
the pond margins, drawn before the pond was enlarged.

**Eastern Roundabout surfacing.** In early July the contractor wrote that the specified
high-skid-resistance aggregate for the roundabout was in short supply and proposed a
lower-grade alternative. A formal submittal followed. The designer accepted it on condition
that high friction surfacing was laid on the approaches; the submittal was approved with
comments at the end of July, and the pavement bill of quantities and roundabout drawing were
revised in August.

**Running the job.** Progress meetings were held roughly every two weeks from spring 2025, with
the same core people, and each covered several of these issues at once. The contractor issued a
monthly progress report and updated the construction programme twice: a recovery programme in
late June and an update in October. In August the contractor gave notice of overnight lane
closures on Kestrel Road for the tie-in, and the council approved the traffic management.
There was also an early question about the bearing plinths at the west abutment, answered and
closed in June.

## Kinds of documents that exist

Drawings, reports, formal questions from the contractor (RFIs), letters, meeting minutes,
submittals for approval, bills of quantities and priced variations, check certificates, and
spreadsheets (the programme and the pile schedule). Some documents have several revisions.
Some have been filed in more than one place, and a few have untidy filenames.
